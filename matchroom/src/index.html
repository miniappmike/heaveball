    New to Heaveball? · Field guide

    How to play

    Two teams of eight attack one shared ring in the middle of the field. Get the ball into the ring to score. The first time you watch, follow the white ball.

    01The field

    1. QuadrantsThrow from here.
    2. RunwayRun or hand off.
    3. Center zoneAnyone can enter. The ring sits inside it.
    4. Dirt stripsOut of bounds for the ball. Passes can fly over them.

    Only Anchors can cross into the opposite half.

    02Scoring

    • 2
      Carry it inRun the ball through the ring.
    • 3
      Catch and finishCatch a teammate's pass in the center zone, then finish through the ring.
    • 1
      Steal in the centerIntercept a pass or recover an opponent’s fumble in the center zone. You also get the ball.
    • 5
      Save-kick catchRare. Catch a legal airborne save kick in the center zone.

    Tied at full time? Sudden death in the runway only. Next score wins.

    03Passing

    • LobThe standard pass. High and arching.
    • BounceSlower and easier to catch. Stays on one half.
    • Line driveFastest and flattest, but harder to catch.
    • UnderhandA safe short toss or handoff to a nearby teammate.

    Weak arms and poor accuracy cause more short-hops, and some throws sail over the receiver.

    04Tackles and restarts

    • Tackled in a quadrantCarrier keeps the ball and gets a standing throw.
    • Tackled in the runwayTurnover.
    • Center catch stopped shortYour team keeps the ball.
    • Incomplete passStill live, unless it lands or rolls out of bounds.
    • Loose ball touches dirt or contact drives the carrier into itTurnover. Opponent restarts in the nearest quadrant.
    • 3+ seconds unblocked in the ringPenalty. Opponent gets a free run lane.

    Substitutions are live and unlimited, but only for the team with the ball. Each club has 12 players: eight on the field and two substitutes on each of its benches. Heavers and Runners come on from the bench on their own half, and Anchors from either bench. The tired player walks off to the end line by the bench, and the carrier usually waits for the substitute to get set. There are no substitutions in sudden death.

    Coaches review the game every five minutes and at halftime. A coach whose defense is leaking points switches to a counter scheme; one whose offense has stalled changes the playbook from the next restart. Each coach's adaptability rating decides how quickly they notice and how often they pick the right answer. Every offense goes to hurry-up in the last 1:30 of the first half, as does a team trailing by five or more in the last five minutes of the game.

    Piles last 2 to 25 seconds. Strength, size and teammates push; Tackling and IQ end them. Carriers can pull out, fumble, sneak a handoff or risk a wild pass. Defenders can also strip the ball.

    Free run lane: the lead Anchor screens the help defender to leave the Runner one-on-one. The first defender can grab and wait for help or try a risky lunge. Every penalty (ring time, block in the back, a Runner or Heaver crossing past their own half, or carrying the ball onto the dirt on your own) awards a free run lane. Scores, penalties and ordinary stoppages freeze play in place for 3.5 seconds so you can read the play log.

    Field guide

    Both teams attack the 4-yard ring inside the 10-yard center zone. A tackle short of the ring stops the finish. Watch for Anchors blocking off the ball, slipping toward the ring or drawing a defender away from a cut. Carry in for two or catch in the blue and finish for three. Watch the white ball and its shadow to read the height of a pass or bounce. Gold outlines show blocks and pile drives. On fumbles, only players with a realistic arrival path pursue; nearby players contest the ball while the rest reposition to cover space or give the recovering player an outlet. A center catch directly from an airborne kick save earns five. New match draws two fresh squads.
