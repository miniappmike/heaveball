# Heaveball Coaching Playbook

Heaveball rewards teams that can hold possession long enough to make a defender choose between two threats. Move the ball through the quadrants, show a credible runway run, and let a player cut into the blue when the defense shifts. Coach the first look and the outlet. Players read the rest of the possession as it develops.

## Field and possession reads

| Situation | What the team should read |
|---|---|
| Ball in a quadrant | The carrier can throw or run. Most quadrant matchups are one-on-one, so clean tackles are rare. A defender can still change the throw by shading the lane into the blue. |
| Ball in the yellow runway | The carrier can run or hand off. A completed tackle turns it over. Help can close once the pass is gone, but someone still has to account for a second carrier and a loose ball. |
| Teammate's pass caught in the blue | A controlled ball breaking the ring line earns 3, either on the catch or on the carry that follows. A tackle short of the ring keeps possession and resets at the center of the nearest quadrant. |
| Carrier enters the blue from outside | A controlled ball breaking the ring line earns 2. A completed tackle short of the ring on the dive gives the ball away. |
| Interception or fumble recovery in the blue | The team securing the ball earns 1 and keeps possession. A loose ball in the yellow is still live, and an incomplete pass can be recovered in bounds. |
| Airborne save | A player out of bounds can kick an airborne ball back into play. Either team can catch the saved kick in the blue for 5. If it lands in bounds, contest the recovery and restart from the center of the nearest quadrant. |
| After scoring 2, 3, or 5 | The opposing team gets possession. The scoring team chooses the restart quadrant. |

Any player can enter the blue on offense or defense. Anchors can cross into the opposite half. Heavers and Runners can each throw from a quadrant, receive, or carry. Their role names describe the skills they typically bring.

## Coaching styles and lineup packages

| Style | How the coach plays | Strength | What opponents can attack |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patient passing** | Work through multiple quadrants, repeat a look to learn how the defense reacts, then hit the unexpected cut into the blue. | Creates 3-point chances and can retain possession after a catch in the blue. | A defense that stays home and tracks late cutters can deny the window. A forced throw can give up a point and possession. |
| **Runway power** | Use Anchor blocks, a strong Runner, and an available handoff option. Pick the moment to test an isolated Anchor. | A stronger carrier can stay upright and drive a pile for several seconds. The threat pulls help toward the runway. | Runway contact makes fumbles a recurring risk. A completed tackle can end a long possession, and crowded handoffs can give defenders a recovery chance. |
| **Balanced** | Show the same setup with a keep, a separate Runner, an outlet, and a switch available as the defense moves. | Can exploit either a pinch or a defense that stays spread. | Needs sharp timing across multiple quadrants. If players read the play differently, the carrier can run out of outlets. |
| **Free Flow** | Give players the freedom to blend quadrant movement, runway threats, Anchor blocks, handoffs, and cuts into the blue as openings develop. | Lets players discover what works against the defense and build on it over successive possessions. | Spacing can break down or possessions can stall if several players make the same read. A targeted call gives the team a clear first action when that happens. |

| Lineup call | Two flexible slots | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Heavy Throw** | Heaver and Heaver | You want more quadrant throwers and several angles into the blue. |
| **Split** | Heaver and Runner | You want an extra thrower on one half and a receiving or carrying threat on the other. |
| **Double Run** | Runner and Runner | You want to threaten carries and short catches from both halves. The starting Heavers handle more of the distribution. |

## Free Flow

Free Flow works like pickup basketball. Players share a few habits and choose their actions as the defense moves. Keep a quadrant outlet available, replace it when someone cuts into the blue, and read where the ball is before committing to a run. Players can borrow actions from any set. A possession can start with a switch, turn into an Anchor block, and finish with a late cut that nobody called.

| What the coach sees | Next possession |
|---|---|
| **Free Flow works.** The team scores or creates the look it wanted, such as a controlled catch in the blue that earns a reset. | Call **“Flow again.”** Keep the freedom to make reads and let players build on the opening they found. |
| **Free Flow stalls.** The team circulates without changing the defense, loses its outlets, or gives up possession on a forced play. | Call **“Set”** and name a targeted play. Use Anchor Iso to test a pinching defender, Blue Window to attack a clear entry into the blue, or Shield Dive to test a favorable runway matchup. |
| **The targeted set restores spacing or exposes a matchup.** | Return to Free Flow if the coach wants players to build on what they learned, or repeat the targeted call while the opening holds. |

Coaches set the threshold. One might call a set after the first empty trip. Another might stay in Free Flow through a turnover if the team is still creating good looks. A coach can also tighten the leash late in a close game or give an experienced lineup more freedom.

## Offensive play calls

| Call | Action and cue | What can go wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **Blue Window** | Throw from a quadrant to a player entering the near edge of the blue. They secure the catch and turn toward the ring. Call it when the thrower sees a clear lane and the cutter can arrive ahead of help. | Any defender can enter the blue and jump the throw. |
| **Second Cut** | Let the first cutter draw attention and exit toward their own half. Send a second player from another angle when a defender follows the first cut or turns away from the second. | Defenders can hand off coverage and keep the second lane closed. The thrower needs an outlet. |
| **Shield Dive** | The attacking Anchor blocks for the Runner. Commit when the block opens a route toward the ring. | Entering the runway commits the ball to a run or handoff. A completed tackle in the runway or short on the dive gives up possession. |
| **Runway Flash** | Show the Runner briefly in the yellow. If a defender steps out, look behind them for a cutter into the blue. If the defender stays deep, take the short catch only if the Runner has a viable carry. | A short catch in the yellow can produce a quick turnover if the Runner is tackled there. |
| **Far Switch** | Throw to a teammate already on the opposite half. A catch in a quadrant preserves the next pass and changes the angle into the blue. | Longer throws give defenders time to react. A catch in the far runway removes the passing option until the ball reaches a legal release area. |
| **Anchor Leak** | Show the usual lead block, then release the attacking Anchor into the blue as a receiver if defenders commit to the Runner. | The Runner loses a blocker. If the defense follows the Anchor, use another outlet. |
| **Runway Relay** | The first carrier draws an Anchor and hands off to a trailing player with a better angle. Keep it if the defense widens early. | A pressured exchange can become a live fumble. The trailing player has to see the same opening. |
| **Drive** | Put the ball in a strong Runner's hands behind an Anchor. Keep a teammate available to take a handoff or recover a fumble while the carrier is still upright and moving. | Prolonged contact gives defenders time to surround the carrier. Extra bodies can also crowd the exchange and leave the offense poorly placed after a turnover. |
| **Second Ball** | Assign nearby support to react to incompletions and fumbles. Keep someone positioned to defend if the opponent recovers. | If everyone chases the loose ball, the opponent can recover with open space. |

## Anchor Iso

The attacking Anchor uses a block to leave a fast Heaver or Runner one-on-one with another Anchor. The defender in the other quadrant on that same half must decide whether to pinch toward the runway. Their movement tells the offense where the next opening is.

**“Two, Iso Three”** starts in Q2 and tests the Q3 defender.

| Cue | Read |
|---|---|
| **Iso Keep** | The Q3 defender stays home and the runner has a favorable angle against the isolated Anchor. Take the lane. |
| **Iso Kickout** | The Q3 defender pinches early. Throw to the open Q3 player before the ball enters the runway. From Q3, read the blue, attack the runway from a new angle, or switch halves. |
| **Hold** | The defender shows help and starts to recover. Keep the ball in Q2 if the lane is unclear. The block may expire while the Heaver waits. |
| **Far Switch** | The defense crowds the whole half. Move the ball through an available quadrant to the far side. |

**Keep Iso** uses the quadrant ball carrier as the run threat. They make the pass-or-run decision before leaving the quadrant. **Runner Iso** keeps the Heaver in the quadrant and sends a separate Runner behind the block. The Heaver can wait longer to see whether help commits before throwing to the Runner, the outlet, or a cutter.

### Four-quadrant assignments for “Two, Iso Three”

| Quadrant | Job |
|---|---|
| **Q2 · Start** | Sell the runway threat and decide whether to keep, hold, or pass. |
| **Q3 · Outlet** | Stay available for the kickout. Read whether the defender can recover in time to contest a throw into the blue. |
| **Q1 · Switch** | Offer a far-half target across the top and become the next thrower if the defense shifts toward Q2 and Q3. |
| **Q4 · Finish** | Time a cut into the blue from the far lower side when the ball moves toward Q1. Stay available in the quadrant if the lane stays covered. |

Flip the assignments when starting from another quadrant. A fast Heaver can sell the keep, a strong second thrower makes the kickout more dangerous, and an instinctive receiver can find the late blue window. The best arrangement depends on those matchups and the angles the defense gives up.

## Defensive play calls

| Call | Action and cue | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **Heaver Press** | Use the quadrant's one-on-one defender to take away the throw into the blue and guide the carrier toward another quadrant. Send help only if the blue and the likely outlet are covered. | Clean tackles are rare in quadrants. If help arrives late, the carrier can throw to the player it just left open, forcing the defense to spread out to recover. A quadrant tackle itself gives the carrier a stationary restart. |
| **Pinch** | One Anchor closes on the carrier in the yellow. The second protects the inside route to the ring and reads the handoff. | A crowded tackle can free the trailing carrier or leave nobody in position for the fumble. |
| **Show and Stay** | The same-side quadrant defender shades toward the runway while staying close enough to contest the outlet. Help once the actual ball carrier commits to the runway. | Against Runner Iso, a separate Heaver may still have the ball in a quadrant. Helping on the fake can open the outlet or a blue cut. |
| **Blue Trap** | Show a tempting lane into the blue and have a second defender ready to step in. Keep someone protecting the ring. | A mistimed jump can give up a 3-point finish. A tackle short after a clean catch still lets the offense keep the ball. |
| **Ring Guard** | Stay between the threat and the ring. If necessary, allow an outer-edge catch in the blue and contest the finish. | The offense may take the short tackle and keep testing the defense from the next restart. |
| **Save Guard** | When the ball heads out, one player reads the potential kick while another covers a catcher in the blue. | Watching the boundary leaves the blue open. If the save lands in bounds, somebody still needs to win the recovery. |

## Sideline cues and game management

- **“Two, Blue Window.”** Start the call from Q2. Name the starting quadrant so players on both halves hear where the first decision will happen.
- **“Give Three.”** After scoring 2, 3, or 5, send the opponent to Q3. Use the restart choice to steer possession toward a favorable defensive matchup or away from their best thrower.
- **“Replace the outlet.”** When a player sees a clean cut into the blue, a teammate fills the passing outlet they left. The Heaver keeps an option if the window closes.
- **“Find the ball.”** On a runway handoff or pile drive, identify who actually has possession before committing help. Keep someone positioned for the fumble and someone between the ball and the ring.
- **“Show it again.”** Repeat a credible block or route to learn who follows it. Once a defender starts anticipating it, use the opening their movement creates. A long possession can change suddenly when a player recognizes the cue.
- **“Flow again” / “Set.”** Keep the players in Free Flow after a productive possession, or name a targeted play when the coach wants to dictate the first action.
- **Protecting a lead.** Use controlled quadrant possession and clean blue entries. Make the restart choice count after a score. Use live substitutions on the half away from the ball while your team has possession when the situation allows.
- **Sudden death.** In runway-only, first-point-wins play, consider a Runner in the flexible slot alongside the Heaver and three Anchors. Pick the best available carrier and use Shield Dive, Runway Relay, Pinch, and loose-ball support. Opening and restart procedures for this format remain to be defined.

## Runway handoff ruling

A run that starts outside the blue continues as the same run if the carrier hands off inside the blue. A controlled ring crossing earns 2, and a completed tackle short of the ring turns it over.
